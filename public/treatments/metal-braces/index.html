Metal braces in Thrissur

Proven, precise and powerful. The most widely used fixed appliance, with excellent three-dimensional control of tooth movement.

Metal braces are small stainless-steel brackets bonded to the front of each tooth, connected by a thin archwire that gently guides the teeth into position.

How they look

Small silver brackets on the front of the teeth, with a wire running across them. Elastic ties hold the wire in each bracket. They come in many colours, which teens often love choosing.

How they work

The archwire applies steady, continuous pressure. At each visit, usually every 4–8 weeks, I adjust or change the wire to keep your teeth moving along the plan.

Why choose them

They’re the most versatile and cost-effective option. They work 24 hours a day without you having to remember to wear anything, which makes them excellent for severe crowding, rotated teeth and complex bites.

Things to know

Avoid hard and sticky foods, brush carefully around the brackets, and expect mild soreness for a few days after each adjustment.

Best suited for

Complex cases, severe crowding and rotations, teens, and anyone who wants reliable results at a lower cost.

What are metal braces?

Metal braces are stainless-steel brackets bonded to each tooth and joined by an archwire. Small elastic ties hold the wire in each bracket, and the wire guides teeth into position over the course of treatment.

The parts

  • Brackets: small anchors bonded to the front of each tooth
  • Archwire: the track that guides movement, changed through stages
  • Ligatures: tiny elastic ties that hold the wire (often the coloured part)
  • Extras when needed: elastics, springs or other appliances to correct the bite

Strengths and trade-offs

Strengths

  • Effective across simple and complex cases
  • Fixed in place, so it works around the clock
  • Excellent control of rotation, torque and vertical position
  • Generally an economical option

Trade-offs

  • Visible on the teeth
  • Extra care to clean around brackets
  • Some foods should be avoided (hard, sticky)
  • Soreness after adjustments, usually mild and short-lived

Living with braces

Cleaning

Brush after meals with a soft brush, add interdental brushes, and clean along the gumline. Good hygiene protects enamel and gums during treatment.

Eating

Cut hard fruits, avoid biting into very hard or sticky foods, and go gently for the first few days after adjustments.

Visits

Review visits are typically every few weeks, depending on your case. If a wire or bracket comes loose, contact us rather than waiting.
