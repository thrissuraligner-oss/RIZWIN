Braces vs clear aligners: why every treatment differs

Same goal, different mechanics. Understanding how each one moves teeth explains why the right choice varies from person to person.

Dr. Rizwin Aligner Orthodontist, ThrissurPending clinical review by Dr. RizwinUpdated 29 September 2026

How each one moves teeth

Clear aligners, metal braces, self-ligating braces and lingual braces all move teeth with gentle, continuous force. They differ in how that force is delivered, whether the appliance is removable, and how visible it is.

Clear aligners: a series of custom trays

Each tray fits your teeth slightly differently from the last, and presses on the teeth that need to move. Attachments give the tray something to grip. Does well: tilting, levelling, spacing, and discretion. Know: results depend on wearing trays 20 to 22 hours a day, and some movements are harder to control.

Metal braces: a wire that guides every tooth

A bracket on each tooth acts as a handle. The archwire, held with elastic ties, applies continuous gentle force and controls how each tooth tilts, rotates and moves. Does well: precise 3D control and complex cases. Know: visible, and cleaning needs more care.

Self-ligating: same idea, different way of holding the wire

A built-in clip holds the archwire, so no elastic ties are needed. The wire still does the work of guiding teeth. Does well: can feel easier to clean and quick to adjust. Know: systematic review evidence has not shown a clear outcome advantage over conventional brackets.[1]

Lingual braces: fixed braces behind the teeth

Brackets sit on the tongue side, so they are hidden. Some systems, such as Brava, use individually designed arms rather than a single archwire, according to the manufacturer. Does well: discreet and fixed. Know: tongue adaptation, cleaning technique and case selection matter.

What decides the right choice?

Your teeth and bite

How far teeth must move, in which directions, and how the upper and lower jaws relate.

Age and growth

Growing children may benefit from guided jaw growth before or alongside appliances.

Your lifestyle

Aligners need consistent wear. Braces need careful cleaning and food choices. Lingual braces need tongue adaptation.

Bottom line. Neither pathway is best for everyone. In non-growing patients with mild to moderate problems, aligners can work very well, and shorter treatment and less chair time have been reported in that group. For larger movements and complex bites, braces often remain the more predictable route.[2,3]
