Why you need a retainer after braces or aligners

Straightening teeth is half the job. Keeping them straight is the other half.

Dr. Rizwin Aligner Orthodontist, ThrissurPending clinical review by Dr. RizwinUpdated 30 October 2026

Quick answer

You need a retainer because teeth tend to move back towards their old positions after braces or aligners, a process called relapse. Retainers, either fixed wires bonded behind the teeth or removable clear or wire retainers, hold teeth in place while the gums and bone settle and as your mouth changes with age.

Why do teeth move back?

After teeth are moved, the gum fibres and bone around them take months to adapt, and teeth keep changing slowly throughout life. Without retention, they tend to drift back towards where they started. The Cochrane review on retention describes this relapse as the reason a retention phase is needed.[1]

Fixed vs removable retainers

FeatureFixed (bonded) retainerRemovable clear retainer
What it isThin wire bonded behind the front teethClear tray like an aligner
WearAlways in placeAs prescribed, often nights long-term
ProsDoes not rely on rememberingEasy cleaning; covers all teeth
ConsCan detach; needs careful flossingCan be lost or worn out; relies on wear

The 2023 Cochrane review found low-certainty evidence and no clear winner: removable clear retainers were linked with better gum health in some studies, while fixed retainers did not depend on patient wear.[1] Many orthodontists combine both.

How long should you wear a retainer?

For most people, retention is long-term. A common approach is full-time wear for a period after treatment, then night-time wear for as long as you want straight teeth. Your orthodontist will set a schedule based on your case.

Caring for your retainer

  • Clean removable retainers daily with a soft brush and cool water; hot water can warp them.
  • Keep them in their case, away from pets and heat.
  • Floss around fixed retainers with a threader or super floss.
  • Report a loose wire or cracked retainer promptly.

What a typical retention plan looks like

  1. Day of debond or last aligner: retainers are fitted, often a bonded wire behind the lower front teeth and a clear retainer for each arch.
  2. First months: removable retainers are worn as instructed, often most of the day and night, while the gums and bone settle.
  3. After that: night-time wear, long-term.
  4. Reviews: retainers are checked for fit and wear; clear retainers are replaced when worn or cracked.

Your orthodontist may change this depending on how much your teeth were moved, your gum health and your age.

Signs your retainer needs attention

  • A removable retainer feels tight after a few nights off, which means teeth have started moving.
  • A crack, split or cloudy, worn surface.
  • A bonded wire that feels rough, loose or has a gap under it.
  • Food or plaque building up around a fixed wire.

Small problems are quick to fix; ignored ones can mean repeating part of your treatment.

Teeth already moved after old braces?

If your teeth have moved after old braces, a short course of clear aligners can often correct mild relapse. An orthodontist checks how much movement has happened and whether your bite has changed, then plans treatment and long-term retainers.

Relapse is one of the most common reasons adults seek aligners. Mild relapse can often be corrected with a short course of aligners. See retainers and how long aligners take.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a retainer forever?

Most orthodontists advise long-term retention, usually at night, because teeth can move throughout life.

What if I stop wearing my retainer?

Teeth may shift. If your retainer still fits, wear it again; if it doesn't, see your orthodontist before it gets worse.

Do aligner patients need retainers too?

Yes. Retention is needed after aligners just as after braces.

Can a fixed retainer break?

Yes, the bond can fail. Contact your orthodontist if the wire feels loose.

Can my old retainer be reused after new treatment?

Usually not. A new retainer is made for your new tooth positions.
