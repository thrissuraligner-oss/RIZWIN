Are clear aligners effective? What the research says

A plain-language summary of the evidence on clear aligners: where they work well, where they struggle, and what that means for you.

Dr. Rizwin Aligner Orthodontist, ThrissurPending clinical review by Dr. RizwinUpdated 30 October 2026

Quick answer

Yes, clear aligners are effective for many cases of crowding, spacing and mild to moderate bite problems when they are planned and supervised by an orthodontist. Research shows they align and level teeth well, may shorten treatment in milder cases and support gum health, but some movements, such as rotating rounded teeth, are less predictable.

What aligners do well

Systematic reviews report that aligners align and level the arches, control front-tooth intrusion and back-tooth tipping, and can move upper molars backwards by small amounts predictably.[1] In mild to moderate cases, they may shorten overall treatment and chair time compared with braces.[2]

Where aligners are less predictable

Extrusion (moving a tooth down out of the gum) and rotation of rounded teeth such as canines and premolars are the hardest movements for aligners.[1][3] A 2020 prospective study found a mean accuracy of about 50% per planned movement, with rotations lowest.[4]

This is why orthodontists use attachments, staged movements and refinement aligners, and why some cases are better treated with braces.

Aligners vs braces: outcomes

In a study of patients with mild to moderate problems, both aligners and braces improved the bite. Aligner patients finished about 5.7 months sooner on average, while braces were more likely to achieve a “great improvement”.[5] A meta-analysis found no differences in stability after treatment, though the evidence base was limited.[2]

Gum health and comfort

Because aligners come out for brushing and flossing, gum health indexes tend to improve during aligner treatment compared with fixed braces.[6] In a randomised trial, adults with braces reported more discomfort in the first week than adults with aligners, and more of them used painkillers.[7]

What this means for you

  • Aligners are a strong option for many adults and teenagers with crowding, spacing and mild to moderate bite problems.
  • Results depend heavily on planning and supervision, not just the product.
  • For some complex bites, braces or combined treatment remain the better tool.
  • Retainers are needed after any orthodontic treatment.[8]

Frequently asked questions

Are aligners as good as braces?

For many mild to moderate cases, outcomes are comparable. For some complex bites, braces are more predictable. The diagnosis decides.

Why do some people need refinements?

Not every planned movement is fully achieved in the first series, so a short set of extra aligners fine-tunes the result.

Are aligners safe for gums?

Research suggests gum health indexes tend to improve with aligners compared with fixed braces, because you can clean normally.

Do aligners work for adults?

Yes. Many aligner patients are adults; healthy gums and bone are what matter.

Is the evidence strong?

It is growing but still limited in quality. That is another reason to choose an orthodontist who adapts the plan to your case.
