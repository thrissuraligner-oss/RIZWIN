What problems can clear aligners fix?

From gap teeth to bite problems: an orthodontist's guide to what aligners handle well, and where they need help.

Dr. Rizwin Aligner Orthodontist, ThrissurPending clinical review by Dr. RizwinUpdated 30 October 2026

Quick answer

Clear aligners can fix crowded teeth, gaps between teeth, mild to moderate overbite and deep bite, many open bites, some crossbites, forward-leaning teeth and relapse after old braces. Severe skeletal problems, large rotations and some extraction cases may need braces, functional appliances or combined treatment.

Problems aligners usually treat well

  • Crowding: space is created by gentle arch shaping, uprighting or IPR, then teeth are aligned.
  • Spacing and gap teeth: gaps are closed in planned stages.
  • Relapse after braces: teeth that drifted back can often be corrected with a short course.
  • Forward-leaning front teeth: when space is available.
  • Mild to moderate deep bite and open bite: with careful staging and attachments.

Problems that need careful planning

Rotations of rounded teeth, moving teeth down (extrusion) and large root movements are less predictable with aligners.[1] They can often be managed with attachments, staging, elastics and refinements, but the orthodontist may suggest braces when predictability matters more.

When braces or other appliances may be better

  • Severe jaw-size mismatches (skeletal Class II or Class III).
  • Growing children who need jaw growth guided, where functional appliances or expanders are used.
  • Some cases needing extractions with large tooth movements.
  • Patients who cannot wear aligners 20 to 22 hours a day.

See myofunctional therapy, expanders and braces.

Crowded teeth with aligners

Crowding is the most common reason people seek aligners. Space is created in planned ways: gently widening the arch where bone allows, uprighting teeth that lean, or polishing tiny amounts between teeth (IPR). Teeth are then aligned a fraction of a millimetre per tray.

Mild crowding often finishes within months; severe crowding may need longer treatment, and occasionally extractions or braces.

Gap teeth and spacing with aligners

Gaps close predictably with aligners in many cases. The plan also checks why the gaps appeared: small or missing teeth, tongue habits or gum changes. If a habit caused the spacing, myofunctional therapy helps keep the gaps closed, and a retainer is essential.

Bite problems: overbite, open bite and crossbite

  • Deep bite (overbite): aligners can level the teeth and open the bite with careful staging and bite ramps.
  • Open bite: many dental open bites close with aligners while the back teeth are controlled; habits must be addressed.
  • Crossbite: single-tooth and mild crossbites often respond; narrow upper jaws in children may need an expander first.
  • Underbite: mild dental cases sometimes; skeletal cases usually need other treatment.

How you find out if aligners suit you

A diagnosis with photos, X-rays and a 3D scan answers it. Try the 30-second candidate check, then book an online consultation for an honest opinion.

Frequently asked questions

Can aligners fix an overbite?

Many mild to moderate overbites and deep bites can be improved with aligners, sometimes with elastics. Severe skeletal overbites may need other treatment.

Can aligners close a gap between front teeth?

Yes, closing gaps is one of the things aligners do well. A retainer keeps the gap closed.

Can aligners fix an underbite?

Mild dental underbites sometimes; skeletal underbites usually need braces, growth guidance or surgery.

Can aligners fix crooked teeth after old braces?

Often, yes. Mild relapse is a common reason for short aligner treatment.

Do I need teeth removed for aligners?

Most aligner cases don't. Some severe crowding cases do; your plan will explain.
