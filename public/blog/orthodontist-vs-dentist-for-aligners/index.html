Orthodontist vs general dentist for clear aligners

Many dentists offer aligners. Here is how an orthodontist's training differs, and when it matters most.

Dr. Rizwin Aligner Orthodontist, ThrissurPending clinical review by Dr. RizwinUpdated 30 October 2026

Quick answer

Both general dentists and orthodontists can provide clear aligners, but an orthodontist has completed an additional three-year MDS in Orthodontics and Dentofacial Orthopaedics focused on diagnosing bites and planning tooth movement. For anything beyond very mild crowding or spacing, especially bite problems, an orthodontist is the safer choice.

Training differences

A general dentist completes a BDS and treats the whole mouth. An orthodontist completes a BDS and then a three-year MDS devoted to tooth movement, jaw growth, bite correction and appliance design, including braces, aligners and functional appliances.

When the difference matters most

  • Bite problems: overbite, underbite, open bite, crossbite.
  • Moderate to severe crowding.
  • Children and teenagers who are still growing.
  • Teeth that have moved after earlier treatment.
  • Cases where aligners alone may not be enough.

Aligner software cannot deliver every planned movement on its own; studies report that only around half of the planned movement is achieved in one round on average, so the clinician's planning and refinements matter.[1]

Questions to ask your aligner provider

  • What is your orthodontic training?
  • Do you plan the movement yourself or accept the software proposal?
  • Do you also offer braces if aligners are not ideal?
  • How will my bite, not just the front teeth, be corrected?

See also how to choose an orthodontist.

What an orthodontist looks at that is easy to miss

  • How the upper and lower jaws relate, not just how the front teeth look.
  • Whether moving teeth forward or outward is safe for the gums and bone.
  • Growth in children and teenagers.
  • Habits such as tongue thrust or mouth breathing that can undo treatment.
  • Whether the result will be stable, and how to retain it.

Signs your case needs an orthodontist

See an orthodontist if your back teeth do not meet properly, your front teeth do not overlap or overlap too much, your jaw clicks or hurts, your child has crowding or a narrow jaw, or teeth moved after earlier braces. These need full bite diagnosis.

How to check an orthodontist's training

Ask for the qualification directly: an MDS in Orthodontics and Dentofacial Orthopaedics, with registration at the State Dental Council. Clinic websites and the about page should name the orthodontist who will plan your treatment. See about Dr. Rizwin.

Working together

Your general dentist remains important: check-ups, cleaning and fillings should continue during orthodontic treatment, and many dentists refer to an orthodontist for aligner planning.

Frequently asked questions

Can my family dentist do my aligners?

Some do, especially for mild cases. Ask about their orthodontic training and who plans the movement.

Is an orthodontist more expensive?

Not necessarily. Compare written, all-inclusive fees.

Do I still need my dentist during aligners?

Yes. Keep regular check-ups and cleaning.

Can an orthodontist give a second opinion on a dentist's plan?

Yes, a second opinion is common and sensible.

What does MDS Orthodontics mean?

Master of Dental Surgery in Orthodontics and Dentofacial Orthopaedics, the specialist degree in India.

Is an orthodontist needed for simple gaps?

Not always, but an orthodontic opinion confirms that the gap is simple and that closing it is stable.
