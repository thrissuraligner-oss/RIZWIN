What age can you get clear aligners?

There is no upper age limit for straight teeth. The right timing depends on growth, gum health and the problem being treated.

Dr. Rizwin Aligner Orthodontist, ThrissurPending clinical review by Dr. RizwinUpdated 30 October 2026

Quick answer

Clear aligners can be used from the teenage years once most adult teeth are in, and there is no upper age limit for healthy adults. Younger children usually need different appliances to guide jaw growth. A first orthodontic check around age 7 helps decide the right timing.

Children (around 7 to 11)

At this age the priority is often guiding jaw growth and correcting habits, such as thumb-sucking, mouth breathing or tongue thrust. Expanders and functional or myofunctional appliances are often more suitable than aligners. A check around age 7 shows whether treatment is needed now or later.

See child orthodontics.

Teenagers

Aligners can work well for teenagers once most permanent teeth have erupted, if they wear them consistently. Braces remain a good choice when wear time is a worry or the bite is complex.

Adults, including over 40

Adults are some of the most common aligner patients. Healthy gums and bone matter more than age. Adults may need gum care first, and treatment may be coordinated with implants, crowns or gum treatment.

See adult orthodontics.

Aligners for teenagers: what parents should know

Teenagers can do well with aligners if they wear them consistently. Parents should ask how wear will be monitored, what happens if aligners are lost, and whether the plan allows for teeth still erupting. Braces may suit teenagers who are unlikely to wear aligners 20 to 22 hours a day.

Adults: what to sort out before aligners

  • A dental check-up and cleaning.
  • Treatment for decay or gum disease.
  • Plans for missing teeth, implants or crowns, so tooth positions can be planned around them.
  • Any jaw joint symptoms.

Gum health tends to be easier to maintain with removable aligners than with fixed braces.[1]

Is it ever too late for orthodontics?

No. There is no upper age limit for orthodontic treatment. What matters is healthy gums and enough bone support around the teeth, so adults in their 40s, 50s and beyond can straighten their teeth with aligners or braces.

No fixed age limit exists. What matters is healthy gums and enough bone support. Adults in their 40s, 50s and beyond often choose aligners because they are discreet and removable.

What decides suitability at any age

  • Gum and bone health.
  • The type and severity of the problem.
  • Ability to wear aligners 20 to 22 hours a day.
  • Growth stage in younger patients.

Frequently asked questions

Am I too old for aligners at 50?

No. Healthy adults of any age can have aligners. Gum health is assessed first.

Can a 10-year-old have aligners?

Some can, but many children of this age need growth-guidance appliances instead. An orthodontic check decides.

Are aligners good for teenagers?

Yes, if they wear them consistently. Braces may be better if wear is a concern.

Why see an orthodontist at age 7?

Some jaw and bite problems are easier to treat while children are growing.

Do adults take longer to treat?

Adult tooth movement can be slower, and adults may need other dental care coordinated.

Can pregnant women have aligners?

Ask your orthodontist and doctor; X-rays and timing may need special planning.
