Do clear aligners hurt? What to expect

Most people feel pressure, not pain. Here is what is normal, how it compares with braces, and how to stay comfortable.

Dr. Rizwin Aligner Orthodontist, ThrissurPending clinical review by Dr. RizwinUpdated 30 October 2026

Quick answer

Clear aligners usually cause pressure or mild soreness for the first two or three days of each new tray, which settles as teeth adjust. In a randomised trial, adults with aligners reported less discomfort in the first week than adults with traditional braces, and fewer of them used painkillers.

What aligner discomfort feels like

Most patients describe a tight, pressing feeling when a new aligner goes in, and teeth that feel tender when biting. This is a sign the tray is working. It usually eases within two to three days and becomes milder as treatment goes on.

Aligners vs braces: which hurts more?

Research suggests braces cause more discomfort than aligners in the first week. In a randomised trial, both groups felt similar discomfort on day one, but braces patients reported more discomfort afterwards and more of them used painkillers.[1]

In a randomised trial of adults, both groups had similar discomfort on the first day, but people with traditional braces reported more discomfort during the first week and after adjustments, and more of them used painkillers.[1] Aligners also have no wires or brackets to rub the cheeks.

Tips to stay comfortable

  • Start a new aligner at bedtime, so the first hours of pressure pass while you sleep.
  • Eat softer foods for the first day of a new tray.
  • Use aligner “chewies” if provided, to help trays seat fully.
  • Smooth a sharp edge with a nail file and tell your orthodontist.
  • Ask your doctor before taking any pain relief.

A typical first week with aligners

DayWhat most people feelWhat helps
Day 1Tightness and pressure, tender bitingSoft foods, start the tray at bedtime
Days 2–3Pressure easing, slight lisp settlingKeep wear time high so trays seat
Days 4–7Usually comfortableNormal routine

Each new tray tends to repeat a milder version of this pattern.

Why aligner discomfort is a good sign

Pressure means the aligner is applying the planned force to move teeth. Orthodontists stage movements in small steps so that force stays gentle. Severe or worsening pain is not expected and should be checked.

Cheek and gum irritation is also less common than with braces, because aligners have no brackets or wires. If an edge rubs, it can be smoothed in a minute at the clinic.

Making the first aligner easier

Before your fitting, plan soft meals for the first day, such as idli, upma, curd rice, soups and well-cooked vegetables. Ask your orthodontist to show you how to remove and insert the trays: once you have the technique, the first days are much easier.

When to contact your orthodontist

  • Pain that gets worse rather than better after a few days.
  • A tray that will not seat or has cracked.
  • A sore spot on the gum or tongue that does not heal.
  • A loose attachment.

Frequently asked questions

Does every new aligner hurt?

Most new trays bring a day or two of pressure, usually milder as treatment progresses.

Do attachments hurt when placed?

No. They are bonded to the tooth surface without injections or drilling.

Does IPR hurt?

Usually not. It is gentle polishing between teeth, typically without needing anaesthetic.

Can I take painkillers?

Ask your orthodontist or doctor which option is suitable for you.

Will aligners affect my speech?

Some people lisp slightly for a day or two; it usually settles quickly.
