Aligner attachments and IPR, explained simply

Two small steps that make a big difference to how predictably aligners move teeth.

Dr. Rizwin Aligner Orthodontist, ThrissurPending clinical review by Dr. RizwinUpdated 30 October 2026

Quick answer

Attachments are small tooth-coloured bumps bonded to some teeth so aligners can grip and move them precisely. IPR (interproximal reduction) is gentle polishing between teeth to create a little space. Both are common in aligner treatment, usually cause no pain, and are planned in advance in your 3D plan.

What are attachments?

Attachments are small shapes of tooth-coloured composite bonded to the tooth surface. The aligner fits over them like a handle, so it can rotate, tip or move a tooth more accurately. They are removed and polished off at the end of treatment.

They help because aligners alone are less predictable for some movements, such as rotations and extrusion.[1][2]

What is IPR?

IPR removes a very small amount of enamel between teeth, usually fractions of a millimetre, to create space for alignment and to reshape contacts. It is planned to stay within safe limits of enamel thickness and is usually done without anaesthetic.

Do attachments show?

Attachments are small and tooth-coloured, so they are hard to notice from conversational distance. Close up, people may see small bumps on some teeth, especially if they pick up stain from tea, coffee or turmeric, which careful brushing helps prevent.

They are tooth-coloured and small. Up close they can be noticed, but from conversational distance most people do not see them.

How attachments are placed

  1. The teeth are cleaned and gently prepared.
  2. A template tray filled with tooth-coloured material is placed over the teeth.
  3. The material is set with a light, leaving small shaped bumps.
  4. Excess is polished away and your first aligner is fitted.

No injections or drilling are needed.

Living with attachments

  • Brush carefully around them; plaque can collect at the edges.
  • Eat with aligners out, especially turmeric-rich curries, tea and coffee, to limit staining.
  • If one comes off, keep wearing your aligner and tell the clinic.

What happens at the end

When active treatment finishes, attachments are polished off and the teeth are smoothed. Retainers are then fitted to hold the result.[3]

Are attachments and IPR always needed?

No. Attachments and IPR are used only when your plan needs them. Simple cases may need few or none, while rotations, root movements and crowding usually need more. Your 3D plan shows exactly where they are placed before treatment starts.

No. Your plan decides. Simple cases may need few or none; complex movements often need more. Ask to see them in your 3D plan.

Frequently asked questions

Do attachments damage teeth?

They are bonded to the enamel surface and polished off afterwards, similar to how brackets are removed.

Does IPR weaken teeth?

When kept within planned limits, IPR is a routine and safe procedure.

Can attachments fall off?

Occasionally. Tell your orthodontist; they can be replaced.

Do attachments stain?

They can pick up some stain, especially with tea, coffee and turmeric. Brushing and eating with aligners out helps.

Is IPR the same as filing teeth down?

It removes only a tiny, planned amount of enamel between teeth, not from the front surfaces.
