Clear aligner and braces questions, answered by an orthodontist

The questions patients search for most, answered honestly.

By Dr. Rizwin, orthodontist and clear aligner specialist, ThrissurUpdated 28 October 2026

Am I a candidate?

Am I a good candidate for clear aligners?

Most teens and adults with crowding, gaps, forward teeth, mild-to-moderate bite problems or teeth that moved after braces are good candidates. What matters most is healthy gums and teeth, and being willing to wear aligners 20–22 hours a day.

How can I check myself before booking?

You’re likely a good candidate if:

  • You’re 12 or older and all your adult teeth have come in
  • Your concern is crowding, spacing, forward teeth or relapse
  • Your gums don’t bleed and your teeth aren’t loose
  • You can commit to 20–22 hours of wear a day

It needs a closer look if:

  • Your upper and lower jaws are very mismatched in size or position
  • Several teeth are missing, or you’re planning implants or crowns soon
  • You have active gum disease or untreated cavities

The self-check above gives you a first answer.

Am I too old for aligners?

There’s no upper age limit. Teeth move at any age as long as the gums and bone around them are healthy. Many of my patients are in their 30s, 40s and 50s.

Can aligners fix my bite, or only make teeth look straight?

Aligners can correct many deep bites, open bites and crossbites, using small attachments, elastics and careful planning. When the problem is in the jaw bones themselves, a functional appliance (for children), fixed braces or combined treatment may work better. I’ll tell you which applies to you.

I had braces before and my teeth moved. Can aligners fix that?

Yes, and this is one of the most common reasons adults come to me. Relapse cases are often short treatments, followed by a good retainer plan so it doesn’t happen again.

How do they work?

How do clear aligners move teeth?

Each aligner is made slightly different from your current tooth position. It applies gentle, constant pressure, and the bone around the tooth slowly remodels to let it move. Every new aligner moves teeth roughly a quarter of a millimetre further along a plan I design in 3D.

What are attachments and IPR?

Attachments are tiny tooth-coloured bumps bonded to some teeth. They give the aligner something to grip, for more precise movements. IPR means polishing a fraction of a millimetre between some teeth to create space. Both are quick and comfortable, and they’re used only when your plan needs them.

How long does treatment take?

Mild cases often take 4–8 months, moderate cases 8–14 months, and complex cases 12–24 months. Wearing your aligners consistently is the biggest factor in finishing on time.

How often do I change aligners and see the doctor?

You usually switch to a new aligner every 7–14 days. You’ll visit the clinic a few times, for the scan, attachments and finishing. Most check-ins happen online, with photos and video calls.

Can treatment be done online?

The consultation, planning and most follow-ups, yes. The 3D scan and placing attachments need a clinic visit, at my clinic or a partner clinic. Your aligners are made in my lab and sent to you.

I live outside India. Can I still consult with you?

Yes. Wherever you are in the world, you can book an online consultation here. We’ll schedule the call in your time zone. If you’re planning to visit India, we can do the consultation and planning before you travel, and arrange your scan and aligner fitting during your visit.

Pain & comfort

Are aligners painful?

Most people feel pressure or tightness for 1–2 days after switching to a new aligner, which is a sign it’s working. It’s usually much milder than braces, with no wires or brackets rubbing your cheeks. If needed, a simple painkiller helps on the first day.

Will I speak differently?

Some people have a slight lisp for the first few days. Your tongue adapts quickly, and most people sound completely normal within a week.

Can people see them?

Aligners are clear and thin, so most people won’t notice unless they look closely. Attachments are tooth-coloured. If you need something completely hidden, lingual braces sit behind the teeth.

Do aligners cause bad breath or sore gums?

Not if you keep them clean. Brush your teeth before putting aligners back in, rinse the aligners whenever you remove them, and clean them gently every day.

Food & daily life

Can I eat with aligners?

You take them out to eat, so there are no food restrictions. Eat what you like, then brush or at least rinse before putting them back in.

What can I drink with them in?

Plain water only. Tea, coffee and other hot drinks can warp or stain aligners, and sugary or acidic drinks get trapped against your teeth. Remove them for anything other than water.

How many hours a day do I need to wear them?

20–22 hours a day, including while you sleep. Aligners only move teeth while they’re in your mouth, so fewer hours means teeth fall behind the plan.

What if I lose or break an aligner?

Tell me right away. Usually you move to your next aligner or go back to the previous one while my lab makes a replacement quickly.

Can I wear them for weddings, travel or sports?

Yes. You can take them out briefly for a big event, and we can plan around important dates. For contact sports, a mouthguard may be advised.

Cost

How much do clear aligners cost?

It depends on how complex your case is and how many aligners you need. You get a written, all-inclusive fee after your 3D plan.

Why are some aligners so much cheaper?

Lower-cost mail-order aligners often skip an in-person examination, X-rays and close doctor supervision. That can work for very mild cases, but corrections for treatment that goes wrong can cost more later.

What’s included in the fee?

All your aligners, your 3D smile preview, attachments and IPR, refinements if needed, and your retainers.

Are aligners worth it compared with braces?

Both work well when they’re planned properly. Aligners cost about the same or a little more, and they’re nearly invisible and removable. Braces suit some complex cases better. I offer both, so I’ll recommend whichever gives you the better result.

Results & after

Will my results last?

Yes, with retainers. Teeth tend to drift back toward where they were, so every plan with me includes a retention phase.

Do I need to wear retainers forever?

Usually full-time for a short period, then at night only, long-term. A few nights a week of retainer wear is a small price for a lifetime of straight teeth.

What if my teeth don’t move as planned?

Sometimes a tooth lags behind, often because wear time slipped. I check your fit regularly, and if needed my lab makes a few extra refinement aligners to finish the job.

Are aligners safe for my teeth?

When they’re planned and supervised by an orthodontist, yes. The main risks come from poor hygiene (cavities under the aligners) or teeth moved too fast without supervision. Both are avoidable.
